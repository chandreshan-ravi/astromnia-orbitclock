# Astromnia Analysis Software (OrbitClock) — tester guide

**Link:** https://astromnia-melatonin.onrender.com/  **Access code:** _(sent separately)_

OrbitClock is a research prototype, not a medical device. Melatonin plans it shows are suggestions for flight-surgeon approval. Never use them for treatment.

## Sign in
Open the link on a laptop, tablet or phone. Enter your name or tester ID and the access code, tick the notice, then **Sign in**. You stay signed in on that device for 30 days.

## Things to try (about 15 minutes)
1. **Results** (opens first): read the 4-step sample kit protocol and try the 1-minute shake timer. Enter a crew code (for example `T-` plus your initials, never a real name), the phase and the time. Click **Load sample scan** or upload your own DPV CSV. Check the melatonin level, the reference-range card and the melatonin dose card, then **Save reading**.
2. **History**: find your reading. You can delete readings you entered. **Trends**: the readings over time per crew member.
3. **Settings**: the calibration line, detection limits and saliva dilution (read-only for testers).
4. **Live ISS light-dark**: is the station in sunlight or orbital night right now? Watch the countdown to the next sunrise and count the sunrises in the last 24 h.
5. **Mission overview**: data source **SIMULATED**. Move the **Mission timeline** slider to **FD18**. Click **CM-B**, then click the brain (or **View dosage result**). You should see a melatonin dose and its timing.
6. **Crew monitoring**, **Biological clock** and **Melatonin laboratory**: plans for every crew member, DLMO, dose time and the evening melatonin curve.
7. **Sensor laboratory**: change the melatonin sensor to the plain carbon electrode. Every body-clock reading should disappear. Change it back.
8. Switch the data source to **RECORDS** to see results from the saved readings.
9. **About, sources & credits** (sidebar): check that the method, data sources and AI-use statement make sense.

## Report what you find
Click **Send feedback** in the sidebar on the page where it happened. Say what you did, what you expected and what happened. Bugs, confusing wording and suggestions are all useful.

## Good to know
- All testers share one dataset, so you will see other testers' readings.
- Settings (calibration, volumes, launch date, reference range) is managed by the study coordinator and is read-only for testers.
- The 3D brain model is large (about 17 MB) and may take a few seconds to appear on mobile data.
- A melatonin dose is suggested only when a crew member has at least two pre-flight evening series and an in-flight series with a clock at least 1 h late.
